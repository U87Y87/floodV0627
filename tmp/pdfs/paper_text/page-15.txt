3.3. Cube computing optimization using AI
The data are organized as tiles that are loaded into distributed computing nodes in
the cube. Different tiles have different CIs in terms of the geoprocessing algorithms
and heterogeneous spatial feature distributions. Load balancing is a challenge in cube
computation, and two methods are commonly used to address this issue: static and
dynamic scheduling. Dynamic scheduling alleviates the load imbalance by monitoring
the status of the clusters and assigning tasks to idle computing units. However, the
advantages of this strategy are limited when the number of tasks is extremely low or
high. Too few tasks may make it difficult to achieve load balance, whereas too many
tasks often result in high communication cost. Static scheduling can divide and group
data into partitions with balanced computational intensity in advance. The accuracy of
the CI assessment is key to the success of this strategy.
