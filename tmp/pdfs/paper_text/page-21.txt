flood disasters in cities. Thousands of people were evacuated and many facilities were
impacted. This case involves raster data (e.g. remote sensing images), vector data (e.g.
facility features), and tabular data (e.g. populations). GeoCube can help to derive flood
inundation data products, impacted facilities, and affected populations using analytics
of these multi-source data.
In the case implementation, the water bodies before and after the disaster are
extracted from Gaofen-1 images, following which the flood inundation product can be
generated by subtracting the pre- and post-disaster data. Subsequently, impacted
facilities such as roads, bridges, villages, and schools, can be extracted using the flood
inundation product. Figure 10 depicts the flood inundation product (red region) and
impacted bridges (red icons). Furthermore, the population data can be used to derive
the affected populations by matching the village name with the vector data. Figure 11
presents the process of extracting the impacted villages and affected populations dur-
ing the flood disaster using Jupyter Notebook. The process includes importing the
GeoCube kernel, multi-dimensional querying, evaluating the impacted bridges and
affected populations, and displaying the results. The kernel contains the core APIs of
the GeoCube, such as getData(). All of these data products and statistical results can
quickly provide informational support for making disaster response decisions.
